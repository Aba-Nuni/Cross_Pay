The Three Problems Blocking African Financial Inclusion

Real barriers that keep families from accessing safe, affordable cross-border payments

1

40% of Africans Are Still Unbanked

Despite rapid technological progress, financial exclusion remains widespread. Nearly 40% of Africans lack access to formal banking services, with 75% of rural women still unbanked. Without access to regulated systems, many rely on informal money transfers, risking losses through unreliable intermediaries and insecure channels.

40% Unbanked across Africa

Source: AfriCatalyst, 2025

2

Banking Apps That Confuse Rather Than Help

Even when people have accounts, many banking apps are poorly designed. Complex interfaces with unclear menus, jargon-heavy language, and buried features intimidate users—especially those with limited digital literacy. Research shows poor UX design is the primary driver of banking app abandonment, not user error. When an app doesn't feel intuitive, people distrust it and stop using it.

❌ Poor UX = Abandoned Accounts

Source: UXDA, 2025

3

Hidden Exchange Rates & Unknown Deductions

The most frustrating problem: you never know how much money will actually arrive. Banks display exchange rates, but the real rate is buried in fine print. Hidden fees, currency markups, and deductions are only revealed after the transfer. A recipient expecting $100 might receive $85—with no clear explanation. This lack of transparency makes cross-border payments feel like a gamble.

Our Solution: Transparency Through Smarter Design

Solving Africa's cross-border payment challenges with user-centered innovation

1

📱 Mobile & Digital Banking Solutions

Mobile money apps like MTN Mobile Money (Uganda) and MyT Money (Mauritius) make financial services accessible without a bank account. They allow users to securely send and receive money, pay bills, and top up airtime, all from their mobile devices. These platforms are instrumental in promoting financial inclusion, particularly among populations without access to traditional banking services.

The apps allow users to:
  • Send & receive money instantly without a bank account
  • Pay bills or buy airtime directly from the app
  • Receive real-time notifications for transactions

Inspired by leading mobile money services in Africa that promote financial inclusion and accessibility

2

✨ Clean, User-Friendly Interface

Simplicity is trust. We will look at some principles that appeal to the alot of people, regardless of technical skills. Large buttons, clear language, visual feedback at each step, and multilingual support ensure no one feels lost.

Design principles followed:
  • Clear, Concise Language: Use simple terms and avoid banking jargon to ensure all users, regardless of their tech-savviness, can understand.
  • Visual Feedback: Provide immediate, intuitive feedback at each step to guide users through the process.
  • Accessible color contrasts and readable fonts
  • Mobile-first design (most African users access via smartphones)
  • Multilingual Support: Offer language options to cater to diverse user bases, enhancing accessibility.

Best practices from KMS Solutions, UXMatters, and WCAG accessibility standards

3

🤖 AI Chatbot for Real Exchange Rates

Banks and fintechs worldwide are using AI to simplify financial calculations. Apps like Wise and Revolut pioneered transparent, real-time rate displays. We're taking this further with an inbuilt AI chatbot that instantly answers: "How much will arrive after all deductions?" Users simply ask, and the chatbot shows the exact breakdown—no hidden surprises.

The chatbot handles:
  • Real-time exchange rate lookups across all African countries
  • Breakdown of every deduction (bank fees, currency markup, intermediary costs)
  • Comparison of rates across different corridors and providers
  • Support in local languages for accessibility

Inspired by Wise, Revolut, and African fintech leaders who prioritize rate transparency
